\begin{abstract}
Plasmids drive bacterial adaptation and are central tools for engineering
microbes, yet the non-coding elements that govern their transfer and
replication --- origins of transfer (\emph{oriT}) and vegetative replication
(\emph{oriV}) --- remain unannotated across the metagenomic majority of
plasmids. Existing annotation methods are reference- and alignment-based, so
they fail on the divergent, host-uncharacterized sequences that now dominate
plasmid databases. We present \model, a genomic language model trained by
masked-nucleotide prediction on the plasmid sequences of the IMG/PR database
with a diversity-aware sampling scheme that captures the breadth of
metagenomic plasmid diversity rather than sequencing depth. Without any
labels, \model learns representations that organize plasmid DNA by function.
For identifying origin-containing regions, a lightweight classifier on its
frozen embeddings generalizes to origins with no detectable homology to the
training data, outperforms reference-based tools and models trained from
scratch, and matches genomic language models up to 85$\times$ larger at
roughly 45$\times$ less inference compute. Applied across IMG/PR, it
annotates putative origins genome-wide and reveals previously undescribed
transfer and replication gene architectures. Interpreting the model shows
that it has internalized the cis-regulatory architecture of \emph{oriT},
\emph{oriV}, promoters and other functional elements at single-nucleotide
resolution. \model offers a scalable, alignment-free route to annotating the
functional elements of diverse and uncultured plasmids.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
%======================================================================
% As in GPN-Star, this is a one-paragraph pointer to the Supplementary
% Information followed by the journal-required statements. The transcribed
% Methods themselves are in "Supplementary Methods" below. The artifacts'
% prose says "(Methods)", and \nameref{sec:methods} resolves to that word
% because the label sits here rather than on the supplementary section.

The Supplementary Information contains Supplementary Text and the details of
the pretraining corpus and sequence clustering, diversity-aware epoch
construction, sequence preprocessing, windowing and tokenization, model
architecture, training setup and inference, reference origin curation,
plasmid databases and origin detection, intergenic regions and plasmid
selection, homology-partitioned cross-validation splits, windowing and class
imbalance, sequence embedders and probes, probe training and layer selection,
calibration and region aggregation, masked-logit inference, positional
information content, functional annotation of plasmids, locus curation and
coordinate verification, the categorical Jacobian, contextual sequence
embeddings and dimensionality reduction, and regulatory motif scanning.
