\subsection*{A window-to-region pipeline scores intergenic regions for origins}
\label{sec:origin}

Having pretrained \model on the diversity of IMG/PR, we asked whether its
frozen representations could identify intergenic regions containing origins
of transfer or replication. The task is to prioritize origin-containing
regions rather than to recover exact origin boundaries, so we trained
lightweight probes on frozen embeddings, without repeating the pretraining
described in \secref{sec:pretraining}.

A probe that scores fixed-length windows does not by itself annotate a
plasmid, because the unit of interest is a region rather than a window. We
therefore built the classifier into a two-stage pipeline
(\edfref{fig:ed-pipeline}, \edtref{tab:origin-params}, \nameref{sec:methods}).
A query plasmid is annotated into coding sequences and intergenic regions;
each region is tiled into overlapping windows scored from frozen \model
embeddings; and the calibrated scores give a probability profile that
localizes signal to part of a region rather than only flagging the region as
a whole. Pooling that profile yields one score per region, making a plasmid's
regions comparable to each other and therefore rankable. A plasmid carrying no
characterized origin is thus reduced to a short, ordered list of candidate
regions, and this is the procedure we later run across IMG/PR
(\secref{sec:genomewide}).

We evaluated the pipeline on curated origins, 112 \emph{oriT} and 224
\emph{oriV} after redundancy filtering, partitioned by all-against-all local
alignment so that held-out origins are dissimilar to those used for training
(\fref{fig:origin}a;
\suppfrefs{fig:supp-clustermap-orit}{fig:supp-clustermap-oriv}). The
pipeline's two stages give two measurements. Window-level detection asks
whether origin-containing sequence can be recovered among a plasmid's
intergenic windows against a strongly imbalanced background, summarized by
average precision, for which a random ranker scores the positive prevalence of
0.048 (\fref{fig:origin}b). Region-level ranking asks how well the
origin-containing region can be prioritized among a plasmid's regions, scored
by mean average precision (mAP) and Precision@1 (\fref{fig:origin}d). Because
the parts were assigned by sequence similarity, 88\% of held-out positive
\emph{oriT} windows and 87\% of \emph{oriV} windows fall at or below the
BLASTN \cite{camacho2009blast} novelty threshold to their split's training positives (\fref{fig:origin}c,
\nameref{sec:methods}). We call these windows novel in this operational sense;
the label does not establish biological novelty.

\subsection*{\model identifies origins of transfer where reference tools fail}
\label{sec:origin-orit}

At the region level, a probe on frozen \model embeddings ranked the
\emph{oriT}-containing region first in most plasmids, reaching a Precision@1
of 0.87 across the five similarity-aware splits (\fref{fig:origin}d, top) and
an mAP of 0.90 (\tref{tab:region-map}). Existing annotation served this task
poorly. oriTfinder2 \cite{liu2025oritdb,li2018oritfinder} reached mAP 0.15, below the 0.35 of a naive baseline that simply ranks a
plasmid's longest intergenic region first, so on this benchmark the reference
tool is worse than a rule that reads nothing but region length. A probe
trained from scratch on one-hot sequence reached 0.51 and a frozen PlasmidGPT
\cite{shao2026plasmidgpt} probe 0.44. Among the pretrained nucleotide models, \model matched or exceeded
Evo2 \cite{brixi2026evo2} probes up to 85$\times$ larger.

The same ordering held at the window level, where \model reached an average
precision of 0.78 against the 0.048 random-ranker floor, ahead of every Evo2
scale and of the one-hot probes, split by split and not only on average
(\fref{fig:origin}b, top; \tref{tab:window-auprc};
\suppfref{fig:supp-prcurves}). PlasmidGPT was the weakest method, falling
below the from-scratch one-hot baselines despite being a pretrained plasmid
model. The pretrained nucleotide models also retained more of their
performance on novel windows than the from-scratch probes did: \model scored
0.78 there against 0.77 for the larger Evo2 models, within split-to-split
variation,

\subsection*{Origins of replication are harder to rank against strong baselines}
\label{sec:origin-oriv}

The \emph{oriV} task has a different shape, because origins of replication
sit in intergenic regions that simpler methods already find fairly often.
Ranking a plasmid's longest intergenic region first reaches mAP 0.66, nearly
twice its \emph{oriT} value, and OriV-Finder \cite{li2025orivfinder}
reaches 0.73, so unlike oriTfinder2 the reference tool here remains
competitive with the language models. Against that background \model reached
Precision@1 0.77 and mAP 0.82 (\fref{fig:origin}d, bottom;
\tref{tab:region-map}), ahead of the from-scratch one-hot probe at 0.69 and
the PlasmidGPT probe at 0.53. The \emph{oriT} result is therefore the stronger
of the two: on \emph{oriV} the margin over strong sequence-intrinsic baselines
is real but modest.

The two evaluation levels separate the language models differently. At the
region level \model and the two largest Evo2 models are indistinguishable by
mAP, their differences well inside each model's split-to-split spread
(\supptref{tab:supp-region-metrics}), whereas at the window level \model leads
more clearly, 0.67 against 0.62 for Evo2-7B (\fref{fig:origin}b, bottom;
\tref{tab:window-auprc}). Robustness to novel windows followed the \emph{oriT}
pattern (\fref{fig:origin}c, bottom), ranking accuracy is broadly stable
across plasmid-length bins (\suppfref{fig:supp-regforest},
\suppfref{fig:supp-maplen}), and calibrated profiles again localize the origin
within its region (\fref{fig:origin}e, bottom;
\edfref{fig:ed-tracks8}e--h).

\subsection*{Inference cost decides the genome-wide deployment}
\label{sec:origin-cost}

Two features of the comparison hold across both origin types. Parameter count
did not translate monotonically into accuracy, since Evo2-40B did not exceed
Evo2-7B at either level on either task (\tref{tab:region-map},
\tref{tab:window-auprc}).

And pretraining helped only where the corpus matched the target: PlasmidGPT is
pretrained on plasmids, but on engineered constructs rather than environmental
ones, and its probe ranked below a from-scratch one-hot convolution throughout.
Neither result is an artefact of probe configuration or of how the reference
tools were run (\edfref{fig:ed-layer}, \suppfref{fig:supp-reftool},
\nameref{sec:methods}).

Combining calibrated probabilities from Evo2-7B and \model by soft voting
exceeded both constituents, reaching mAP 0.93 on \emph{oriT} and 0.86 on
\emph{oriV} (\tref{tab:region-map}, \tref{tab:window-auprc}). The gain is
consistent with partly non-redundant predictive information in the two models,
which we take up in \secref{sec:discussion}. That ensemble is a benchmark
result; the genome-wide predictions used downstream were generated with
standalone \model, because Evo2-7B carries roughly 15 times as many
parameters and scored a region 45$\times$ more slowly, 49.8 ms against
2,231.7 ms (\fref{fig:origin}f, \nameref{sec:methods}). Under the available
compute budget that throughput difference decided the deployment: we applied
the standalone \model classifiers across IMG/PR to generate genome-wide origin
predictions, which form the basis for the mobilization and replication
analyses in \secref{sec:genomewide}.
