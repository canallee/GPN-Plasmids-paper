\subsection*{Pretraining internalizes origin architecture}
\label{sec:interpretation}

Having applied the \model classifiers across IMG/PR
(\secref{sec:genomewide}), we examined the frozen pretrained model itself, to
ask which recurring sequence dependencies it had acquired during pretraining.
We measured these with a categorical Jacobian, a finite-difference measure of
how far the model's prediction at one site depends on the identity of another
(\nameref{sec:methods}, \supptref{tab:supp-interp-params}), using the
construction developed to extract co-evolutionary signal from protein and
genomic language models \cite{zhang2024protein,cornman2025omg}.
The resulting dependency map is a property of the frozen model's predictive
distribution. It is not a measurement of physical contact, base pairing, or
evolutionary covariation, and no annotation enters the computation.

Two visual signatures recur across the loci examined. Direct repeats are
associated with off-diagonal bands running parallel to the main diagonal at
the repeat period, and inverted repeats with anti-diagonal patterns centred on
the paired arms. \fref{fig:interpretation} shows two characterized case
studies. At the minimal replication origin of \emph{Escherichia coli} plasmid
pSC101, the three 21 bp RepA iterons DR1--DR3 are associated with a parallel
off-diagonal ladder in which the DR1$\times$DR2 and DR1$\times$DR3 bands are
separately resolved, while the inverted repeats IR1 and IR2 show
anti-diagonal patterns; the 11 bp spacer between the second and third iterons,
reported to tolerate substitution without loss of origin function, is
comparatively featureless (\fref{fig:interpretation}b). At the origin of
transfer of \emph{Klebsiella pneumoniae} plasmid pIGRK, IR1, IR2 and IR3 each
show a discrete anti-diagonal pattern and the 12 bp direct repeat DR1 a
compact on-diagonal block, against a comparatively featureless background;
signal also occurs across the P\emph{mobK} $-$35/$-$10 box pair
(\fref{fig:interpretation}a). In both panels the model-derived dependency
patterns align with curated repeat and promoter annotations.

The association extends to further locus classes. Eight additional origins,
four of transfer and four of replication, the latter spanning three
architectural classes, show the same correspondence between repeat geometry and
dependency-pattern geometry
(\suppfrefs{fig:supp-orit-maps}{fig:supp-oriv-maps}). Across four predicted
$\sigma^{70}$ promoters, off-diagonal signal is present between the $-$35 and
$-$10 boxes, the Shine--Dalgarno region carries a dense on-diagonal block, and
the coding sequence shows periodic on-diagonal beading at codon spacing
(\suppfref{fig:supp-promoter}). Long periodic direct-repeat arrays behave
comparably: in four CRISPR arrays the direct repeats are associated with a
periodic off-diagonal lattice at the repeat spacing
(\suppfref{fig:supp-crispr}). We include the CRISPR panels as evidence that
the direct-repeat association is not confined to origins; they are not origin
machinery and carry no implication for \emph{oriT} or \emph{oriV} biology.

These maps report model sensitivity, not biology, and pattern intensity does
not rank functional importance. At pIGRK the outer inverted repeat IR1, which
published deletion analysis reports as dispensable for conjugative transfer,
carries a prominent pattern, while IR2, whose left arm is reported as
mandatory, does not carry a more prominent one (\fref{fig:interpretation}a).
At the NAH7 origin of transfer the inverted repeat IR2 shows essentially no
anti-diagonal signal even though it is a perfect palindrome in the reading
frame used (\suppfref{fig:supp-orit-maps}c). We report that as an exploratory
negative rather than smoothing it over.

One interval carries a pattern without a corresponding annotation. At pIGRK,
a 33 bp interval shows an anti-diagonal pattern consistent with
self-complementarity but is not annotated in the source literature
(\fref{fig:interpretation}a, IR4). Its arms match at 9 of 10 positions, and
its loop contains two of the four 5$'$-AGCGA-3$'$ motifs that the source
proposes as MobK recognition coordinates. The interval was identified after
inspecting the dependency map rather than by a prespecified detector, and it
does not reach significance against a shuffled null (\nameref{sec:methods}).
It may motivate a hairpin hypothesis, which we state as explicitly
unconfirmed: the categorical Jacobian does not measure physical folding, and
testing the hypothesis would require structure probing and mutagenesis
separating the stem from the AGCGA sequence itself. A comparable uncurated
pattern occurs in the pHTbeta origin of transfer
(\suppfref{fig:supp-orit-maps}a).

Embeddings from the same frozen model also organize plasmid sequence by
function. A two-dimensional UMAP \cite{mcinnes2018umap} projection of 308,478 10 bp tiles from 40 origin-enriched Enterobacterales
plasmids shows associations with coarse functional annotation, with a finer
annotation vocabulary, and with GC content measured on the same tiles
(\edfref{fig:ed-umap-feature};
\suppfrefs{fig:supp-umap-detailed}{fig:supp-umap-gc}). We describe association
rather than class separation, and the projection is offered as descriptive
evidence rather than as an account of classifier behaviour, since it is drawn
from a different layer of the model than the origin classifier
(\secref{sec:origin}, \nameref{sec:methods}).

Together these selected examples show that pretraining on plasmid sequence
alone yields recurring model-derived dependencies associated with repeat
periodicity, self-complementarity, promoter organization and periodic
direct-repeat arrays, and that the same maps can flag candidate intervals
that carry no existing annotation and can be tested experimentally.
